It knows when a task is impossible

AWM knows when a task is impossible, provably. And when it becomes possible, it proves that too.

On levels sealed one unit below their cheapest win, every AWM run proves in Lean that no route wins under its learned model. Without AWM, Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra give no proof; most of their runs are undecided or report a wrong bound. Four of AWM's six first bounds equal the true cost; after feedback that a cheaper win exists, all six do.

A sealed level is a game level whose budget has been lowered to one unit below its cheapest win. The route playing here is that cheapest win, found by exhaustive search on the game engine and replayed on it: with a budget of 18 it runs out one action before the goal; with 19 it wins.

How AWM works

  1. 01 Explore & Prototype

    AWM explores the game and writes each guess about its rules as a concept: an executable Python hypothesis, tested against every observed transition and revised when one disagrees.

  2. 02 Structure & Simplify

    It organizes the concepts into typed objects and shared rules, and simplifies them with transformations inspired by category theory. In lp85, eleven concepts about four kinds of button become one rule, press_permutes.

  3. 03 Formalize & Prove

    It encodes the model in Lean 4 and proves kernel-checked theorems about it: that each level is won by its certified run, and, on sealed levels, that no route wins within the budget.

What the Lean proofs establish

  • For each game, that the learned rules agree with every recorded transition, as read by the agent's parser, and that the certified run, recomputed from the first screen by those rules, wins every level.
  • For each sealed level, that no sequence of actions wins within the budget under the learned model.
  • They do not show by themselves that the model is the game. Turning screens into states happens in Python, outside Lean, and a model can be stricter than the game. Evidence that the models match the game comes from outside the proofs: frozen rules predict all 4,160 transitions on unseen layouts, and exhaustive search on the engine confirms the sealed thresholds.

Check it yourself

Each game page offers the agent's files and the steps to re-run its Lean check. A run takes from one to ten minutes and up to about twelve gigabytes of memory, depending on the game.

  1. Download a game's archive from its page.
  2. Install Lean with elan, at the version named in the archive.
  3. Run Lean on the model file. It checks the rules against every recorded transition, recomputes the certified run step by step, and reports the axioms it used.
