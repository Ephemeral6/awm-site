Reasoning about impossible tasks

When a game cannot be won

An agent that keeps failing has not shown that a task is impossible. Here, AWM learns rules from play and uses Lean to prove that no action sequence can win under its model.

Two puzzle levels have budgets just below the cost of their cheapest win. Explore the tasks, follow the agents' reasoning, and inspect the models and proofs behind their answers.

01 · The tasks

The same mechanics, a budget that cannot suffice

In sc25, a block must reach an exit. Clicking patterns on a 3 × 3 panel resizes the block, teleports it, or fires a ray that removes matching gates. Actions spend budget; fuel restores ten units. These levels rearrange walls and objects while retaining the public game's movement, spells and cost rules.

Levels 1–3 introduce the three spells. Levels 4–5 are the evaluation tasks. View the annotated routes ↗

A witness wins at the minimum budget and fails one unit below it; exhaustive search finds no alternative win at the sealed budget. Action counts differ from budget costs: spell completion adds a charge, fuel refunds ten, and the winning move is free. These witnesses minimize the required budget, not the unconstrained number of actions.

Why inspect the rules as well as the proof?

A Lean proof establishes a statement about the learned model. A valid proof can still rely on a rule that misstates the game. Two historical runs overestimated the winning cost; after feedback that a cheaper win existed, they revised a fuel or shooting rule and proved again. The records below keep the initial answers and subsequent repairs separate.
